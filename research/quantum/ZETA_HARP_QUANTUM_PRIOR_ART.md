## The 13 search areas (the program)

1. Quantum simulation proposals for the Riemann zeta function and the Hardy Z
   function (state-engineering and spectral approaches).
2. The Riemann-Siegel formula on quantum hardware: any proposal to evaluate or encode
   the main sum, its phases theta(t) - t ln n, or its truncation window.
3. Amplitude encoding of number-theoretic sequences (n^(-s) weights, Dirichlet
   coefficients, arithmetic functions) into qubit/qudit registers.
4. Overlap and inner-product estimation via Hadamard test / swap test for structured
   classical sums (as opposed to generic states).
5. Qutrit and general qudit register encodings; ternary quantum logic; claimed
   advantages and their critiques.
6. Tensor-network (MPS/TT) compression of oscillatory and arithmetic vectors;
   quantics tensor-train representations of functions.
7. The Berry-Keating xp program and semiclassical models of the Riemann zeros.
8. Hilbert-Polya operator constructions and spectral realizations of zeta zeros.
9. Quantum-chaos statistics of the zeros (Montgomery pair correlation, Odlyzko
   numerics, GUE universality) — as context, not as support.
10. Zeta-function appearances in QFT/string contexts, including zeta regularization
    and the Veneziano-amplitude line already carried in the canon (LIT-RH004).
11. Analog and physical realizations of zeta-related interference (photonic,
    trapped-ion, cold-atom experiments realizing truncated zeta sums or Riemann
    dynamics).
12. Variational / machine-learned representations of Z(t) and of exponential sums.
13. Complexity literature on evaluating exponential sums and the Riemann-Siegel /
    Odlyzko-Schonhage algorithms (the classical baseline any quantum formulation must
    be measured against).
