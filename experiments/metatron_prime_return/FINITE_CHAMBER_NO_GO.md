# Finite-chamber obstruction and the surviving bounded-infinity route

## Exact proposition

Let a metric graph have finitely many edges with fixed positive lengths
`l_1, ..., l_E`. Every periodic orbit in the graph has length

`L_gamma = n_1 l_1 + ... + n_E l_E`,

where each `n_j` is a non-negative integer counting traversals of edge `j`.
Consequently, all periodic-orbit lengths lie in a rational vector space of dimension at
most `E`.

The set `{log p : p prime}` is linearly independent over the rationals. Indeed, a finite
rational relation can be cleared of denominators to give

`a_1 log p_1 + ... + a_m log p_m = 0`

with integer `a_j`. Exponentiation gives

`p_1^(a_1) ... p_m^(a_m) = 1`.

Unique factorization forces every `a_j = 0`.

Therefore no fixed finite metric graph can have a distinct periodic-orbit length equal to
`log p` for every prime. This rules out the literal 13-center graph, and every other
finite-edge graph, as an exact prime-orbit realization of the zeta explicit formula.

This does **not** rule out an infinite graph, a smooth chaotic flow, a non-local operator,
or an arithmetic/noncommutative space. It also does not rule out using a finite graph as a
finite-window approximation.

## What the experiment established

- **MEASURED:** the connected Kirchhoff chamber has one constant zero mode. This is the
  mathematically coherent version of `1` as the neutral foundation, but it is generic.
- **VOID as prime evidence:** the golden deformation scored worse than all 400 frozen
  matched random controls on the finite simple-cycle `log p` test.
- **MEASURED:** the spectral counting slope approaches the linear finite-graph Weyl law.
  It does not have the Riemann-von Mangoldt `T log T` growth.
- **MEASURED, prediction failed:** the unsplit low spectrum was closer to Poisson than to
  GOE or GUE. Golden coordinate deformation retains reflection symmetries, so a mixture of
  symmetry sectors is a plausible explanation, not an established one.

## What “bounded infinity” must mean if the intuition is retained

The outside picture may be bounded while the internal return language has infinite
rational rank. A compact chaotic billiard illustrates the distinction: it occupies a
bounded region but can possess infinitely many primitive closed trajectories of unbounded
intrinsic length. That is closer to the surviving idea than a finite network of tubes.

A viable zeta candidate would need all of the following at once:

1. A fixed Hilbert space and a genuinely self-adjoint operator `H`.
2. A unitary evolution `exp(-itH)`; no energy-dependent rule silently changing the
   operator being diagonalized.
3. A constant/neutral state if desired, cleanly separated from the positive spectrum.
4. Infinitely many primitive returns with independent actions `log p`, without importing
   zeta-zero ordinates.
5. Repeated returns with lengths `m log p` and explicit-formula amplitudes proportional to
   `log(p) p^(-m/2)`, including the correct signs/phases.
6. The Riemann-von Mangoldt spectral count rather than a fixed finite-graph Weyl law.
7. Broken time-reversal symmetry or another justified mechanism for the observed GUE
   spectral statistics.
8. A trace or positivity identity strong enough to force every spectral value to be real;
   numerical resemblance is not sufficient.

## The next mathematical object

The next search target is therefore not a larger Metatron graph. It is a **bounded return
system with infinite symbolic dynamics**: for example, a compact quotient, billiard, flow,
or noncommutative/arithmetic space whose primitive closed trajectories are generated by a
rule rather than individually assigned. The first discriminator is whether its primitive
length spectrum produces `log p` and the correct amplitudes while pseudo-prime and
length-permutation controls fail.

This restates the human intuition in testable terms: the wall may be finite to look at,
but its language of returns cannot be finite.
