# D12 — where do the primes become load-bearing? (predictions before compute, 2026-09-11)
Connes–Consani (Selecta 2021) prove Weil positivity structurally for supp f ⊂ [−log2/2, log2/2] (L₀ = 0.34657), where no prime shift overlaps the support: there W = archimedean + pole exactly. Question: how far beyond L₀ does the prime-free form A(f) + Π(f) stay positive on its own, and where must the primes carry the load?
Test (floating numpy, exploratory, d8 machinery, T = max(1.3·T_env(L), 60), Legendre basis, both parities): for L on a grid 0.30…0.80 step 0.025 compute λmin of (a) the full reduced form R_T (all terms), (b) the prime-free form (archimedean + pole, same β envelope with B = 0), (c) archimedean only. Report the first L where (b) turns negative per parity: L_c(even), L_c(odd).
PREDICTED: (b) ≥ 0 for every L ≤ L₀ in both parities (consistency with the C–C theorem; a violation would mean a normalization error in our form and would be the more important finding). L_c(odd) ∈ (0.40, 0.60); L_c(even) ∈ (0.45, 0.65); L_c(odd) < L_c(even) (D10's arch-only odd control at 0.7 is more negative, −0.74 vs −0.26). (a) stays positive on the whole grid except possibly threshold-T artifacts, guarded by the 1.3 margin. (c) archimedean alone: negative at every L (pole is needed everywhere), PREDICTED.
Kill: if (b) is negative below L₀, VOID the test until the form is re-audited against C–C's normalization.
